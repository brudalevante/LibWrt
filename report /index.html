Technical incident report · Linksys MX4200v1 · 13 July 2026

ath11k RX page-frag memory amplification

A bounded Wi-Fi receive inventory was retaining an expanding physical footprint because unrelated buffer lifetimes shared 32 KiB backing pages. An integrated ath11k candidate stopped the measured collapse without changing required broadcast traffic; the converging ownership, allocator, simulation, and live evidence supports per-RXDMA-ring cache isolation as the primary mechanism, while an included DMA-alignment correction was not independently A/B tested.

01

Overview

The operational failure, the corrected diagnosis, and the recorded outcome.

The router repeatedly lost nearly all available memory, killed Wi-Fi userspace processes, and eventually entered out-of-memory and watchdog recovery. One ath11k reload raised MemAvailable from 3,348 to 170,820 KiB—a 163.5 MiB increase—so the problem was initially described as an “ath11k leak.” The final evidence found a more specific mechanism: logical ownership remained bounded, but physical page-frag backing grew through lifetime mixing and page stranding.

Pre-fix decline 12–16

MB/min approximate sustained MemAvailable loss in natural-load sawtooths.

Fixed inventory 15,357

Posted buffers: 3 × 4,095 regular RX plus 3 × 1,024 monitor.

Mixed pages 86–87%

Lower bound among final tracker-visible pages in two independent cycles.

Post-fix gate 30m + 5m

Bounded memory and page-frag backing, with zero memguard reloads.

Terminology correction. “Leak” remains useful for the operational symptom, but the decisive tracker did not find an unbounded skb population or a growing reaped-but-not-released queue. The failure was allocator amplification: a small survivor could retain an otherwise sparse order-3 page while the ring continued rotating a fixed number of buffers.

System in scope

  • Linksys MX4200v1, qualcommax/ipq807x, approximately 372 MiB RAM.
  • OpenWrt 25.12.4/r32933 with Linux 6.12.87 and backports ath11k/mac80211.
  • Repeater topology: managed STA uplink, two AP interfaces, br-lan, and relayd.
  • Natural traffic includes required ARP, DHCP, multicast, and broadcast forwarding.

Operational symptom

  • Slab and vmalloc stayed comparatively flat while MemAvailable collapsed.
  • __page_frag_cache_refill backing rose approximately 1:1 with the loss.
  • Memory sometimes returned in batches, and ath11k reloads released the accumulated backing.
  • Immediately before final testing, stock behavior reached 7,164 KiB and killed hostapd/wpa_supplicant.
02

Mitigations

Controls that kept the router usable or reduced pressure while the root cause was unresolved.

During validation, two mitigations remained in place: memguard as an OOM backstop and persistent rx-gro-list=off as a traffic/allocator-pressure reduction. Neither is the root kernel fix, and the report does not use either one as proof that the final patch works.

Mitigations used during the recorded experiment and their boundaries
Control Status What it does What it does not do
ath11k memguard Validation backstop Checks every 30 s and reloads ath11k when MemAvailable < 20,000 KiB, subject to boot grace and a 300 s minimum gap. It prevented OOM/watchdog loops. Reloading causes roughly 20–30 s of wireless disruption. It recovers memory but does not correct allocation behavior.
rx-gro-list=off Validation reduction A static gro-toggle utility and net hotplug rule apply the setting to bridge, Ethernet, STA, and AP interfaces across PHY renumbering. It did not flatten memory. Later generation-safe analysis showed that early “74% leak reduction” attribution was not reliable; treat this as reduced churn/retention only.
Hardware watchdog Active safety Provides last-resort recovery if the router becomes unresponsive. It is not part of memory reclamation and should not normally fire.
Deployment rollback guard Validation safety Kept stock modules on disk and automatically rolled back on identity, memory, network, Wi-Fi role, watchdog, memguard, timeout, or fatal-kernel-log failure. It changes no allocator behavior; it made a risky remote A/B test recoverable.

03

Methodology

How the investigation moved from allocator attribution to generation-safe ownership proof and live validation.

GROUND TRUTH

Allocator profiling

/proc/allocinfo isolated __page_frag_cache_refill as the growing byte class. A 100.31 MB increase matched a 100.5 MB availability loss while other callsites stayed flat.

CONFOUND CHECK

Refiller ≠ owner

Allocinfo and page_owner name the code that refills a shared cache, not necessarily the fragment that later retains the page. Allocation attribution could not identify the holder.

FREE ACCOUNTING

Correct false positives

Legacy traces omitted ieee80211_free_txskb, reused skb pointers across generations, and contained concatenated/truncated records. Their bridge/mac80211 leak attribution was withdrawn.

FRAGMENT LIFETIME

Validate final release

fragdiag paired 17,107 of 17,138 observed final skb_release_data(dataref=1) events with page_frag_free within 2 ms. No final-unref defect was found.

RX OWNERSHIP

Track state in-kernel

rxown assigned generations and followed allocate, post, reap, fragment release, IDR occupancy, ring identity, and physical-page history without bulk trace output.

FUNCTIONAL PROOF

Guarded A/B deployment

A transient candidate had to pass fixed endpoint, range, and slope gates twice while module identity, Wi-Fi roles, reachability, watchdog, memguard, and kernel logs stayed healthy.

Two generation-safe ownership cycles

The decisive tracker recorded two independent memory-growth cycles. Across all 26 snapshots, allocations equaled posts, reaps equaled final fragment releases, IDRs stayed bounded, no current ALLOCATED or REAPED backlog remained, and both critical_failures and probe misses were zero.

Interpretation boundary. The 86.47% and 87.25% figures describe final tracker-visible pages with current or historical cross-origin evidence. They are not percentages of all allocinfo pages, proven wasted bytes, or promised savings. “Tracker-visible slack” is unique backing minus aligned bytes of fragments visible to the tracker; invisible pre-load fragments mean it is neither measured free space nor proven waste. Early backing growth also includes discovery of pre-existing, right-censored pages. Historical cross-origin evidence proves that mixing occurred, not that mixing caused every affected page’s sparsity. The captures selected cache isolation as the primary mechanism; the live A/B established sufficiency only for the integrated candidate under this workload.

Deterministic userland simulator

A discrete-event model implements the relevant Linux 6.12 page-frag allocation, bias, refcount, reuse, fallback, and drain behavior. An identical frozen event stream is replayed through shared-cache, per-ring, same-ring-only, and non-page-frag policies. It validates the mechanism and its direction; it is not a firmware or complete router emulator.

Fixes: what changed, what it fixes, and how

The functional ath11k changes, their intended invariants, and their attribution limits.

Before dev_alloc_skb()
→ shared per-CPU page_frag_cache
→ RX / monitor / CE lifetimes mixed
→
After ath11k_dp_rx_alloc_skb(rx_ring)
→ rx_ring->frag_cache
→ one RXDMA ownership domain per cache
PRIMARY MECHANISM · PATCH 949

Private page-frag cache per RXDMA ring

struct dp_rxdma_ring now owns a struct page_frag_cache. Regular RX and monitor-status allocations use the owning ring; monitor-destination allocation intentionally follows the data ring it reuses. CE/HTC remain on the shared cache.

Fixes: new fragments from unrelated RX rings and CE/shared users can no longer occupy the same backing page, removing the measured cross-origin lifetime mixing. Does not guarantee: elimination of different lifetimes within one ring.

ALLOCATOR CONTRACT

ath11k ring-local skb allocator

The helper retains GFP_ATOMIC | __GFP_NOWARN, conditional __GFP_MEMALLOC, NET_SKB_PAD, SKB_HEAD_ALIGN, build_skb(), and page_frag_free() on build failure.

Fixes: cache ownership without weakening memory-pressure, padding, head-frag, or allocation-failure semantics.

LIFECYCLE ORDER

Drain after ring teardown

The cache is drained only after ring-owned skbs have been DMA-unmapped and freed and the ring IDR has been destroyed. Existing SRNG locking serializes normal cache mutation.

Fixes: releases the cache’s retained page/bias safely, without draining while posted buffers still reference it.

RECOVERY SAFETY

Quiesce IRQ/NAPI before crash teardown

Non-reset crash recovery now invokes ath11k_hif_irq_disable(ab) before data-path teardown. Explicit reset and normal destruction already quiesced the path.

Fixes: prevents receive refill activity racing with buffer free and cache drain during recovery.

DMA CORRECTNESS

Use skb_reserve() for empty-skb alignment

Two paths used skb_pull() while skb->len == 0; a positive pull is rejected and cannot move the data pointer. skb_reserve() now applies the delta. The existing 128 extra bytes cover the maximum 127-byte reserve.

Fixes: the intended RX DMA alignment. It was integrated into the live candidate, so its independent contribution to memory behavior was not A/B isolated.

Validation marker, not a functional fix. Patch 950 exposes read-only private_rxfrag=Y. It identified the controlled marker-bearing build because that build applied patch 950 after patch 949. The marker alone proves neither that patch 949 is present nor that it works, and it does not alter allocation or memory behavior.

Deployment scope

The final change required only a rebuilt ath11k.ko. The companion ath11k_ahb.ko was rebuilt and verified for compatibility but remained byte-identical to stock. No additional full-kernel flash was required for the recorded deployment.

Superseded findings and rejected culprits

A cohesive report must preserve what was disproved, not only the final successful hypothesis.

Important investigation corrections
Earlier conclusion Why it appeared plausible Final verdict
Bridge/mac80211 group-frame skbs leaked indefinitely Pointer-only traces did not count ieee80211_free_txskb and confused reused skb addresses. Refuted. Strict replay found zero old bridge/mac80211 generations once the free set and generations were corrected.
UDP multicast local-delivery clones were a separate leak Objects unresolved at a capture boundary were treated as permanently live. Not supported. Boundary censoring and pointer reuse invalidated the inference.
Firmware TX completion / txbuf_idr was dominant Reload recovery and group traffic made firmware ownership a credible candidate. Refuted as the dominant source. IDR populations stayed bounded/nearly empty and the attempted reaper did not explain backing growth.
skb_release_data skipped the final page-frag free Physical backing grew despite apparently short skb lifetimes. Refuted in the measured sample. Final dataref releases paired with page_frag_free as expected.
Regular or monitor RX inventory grew without bound Allocinfo/page_owner attributed growing pages to high-rate ath11k refillers. Refuted. IDRs were fixed at their ring capacities and ownership transitions balanced.
rx-gro-list=off fixed approximately 74% of leaks Legacy old-pointer counts fell after GRO-fraglist activity disappeared. Overstated. The setting reduces churn and remains useful, but the exact percentage relied on analyzers later shown unsafe.
Documentation precedence. Earlier attribution based on allocation call sites and pointer-only skb traces was withdrawn. The authoritative public source set is patch 949, the generation-safe ownership summaries, the deterministic simulator, the corrections in this report, and the recorded live validation.

Stale collector output after the final system upgrade was not treated as post-fix failure evidence. The guarded router observations above were independent of that collector.

Related work and credit

Earlier ring-size mitigations, their evidence, and the boundary of this repository's contribution.

Ring reduction is legitimate prior art for keeping ath11k usable on memory-constrained devices. It provides headroom by reducing logical inventory; it does not by itself prove that ring occupancy grew or identify which surviving fragment retained a shared page.

Prior work and relationship to this investigation
Work Contribution Relationship
hzyitc, 2024 Reduced ath11k TX, RX, and monitor rings; later proposed an OpenWrt small-buffers variant. Earliest ring-reduction implementation located in this review.
Yanko Yankulov, 2026 Independent, aggressive RX-ring reductions tested on a 256 MiB IPQ5018 device. Field validation of ring shrinking as a low-memory trade-off.
EnSpect, 9 July 2026 MX4200/IPQ8074 page-allocator attribution and a gentler ring-shrink deployment. Direct prior art for this device symptom and mitigation; published before this investigation's recorded start.
This work, 11–13 July 2026 Generation-safe ownership accounting, cross-origin lifetime evidence, deterministic policy model, and per-ring page-frag caches. Later mechanism refinement and a distinct fix that preserves stock ring capacity.
Patch boundary. No implementation lines are shared with the earlier dp.h-only size patches. EnSpect's patch should not be copied verbatim: it halves DP_RXDMA_REFILL_RING_SIZE, which this ath11k source sends to firmware as the receive-buffer byte size rather than using it as an SRNG capacity.
08

Limitations and residual risk

What the evidence does not establish and why operational backstops were retained during validation.

  • Workload scope: the live result proves sufficiency for the measured natural repeater traffic, not every possible traffic mix or future firmware.
  • Within-ring stranding: private caches eliminate cross-ring/CE mixing but cannot prevent every temporal lifetime difference inside one RXDMA ring.
  • Integrated alignment change: the skb_reserve() correction shipped in the same candidate, so there is no independent alignment-only A/B attribution.
  • Simulator scope: results validate mechanism and sensitivity; synthetic lifetimes and source correlations are not a live memory forecast.
  • Legacy analysis tooling: the older pointer-only Python analyzers were not suitable for publication as decisive evidence. The included rxown tracker itself is generation-safe.
  • Backstop: memguard remained enabled during validation. Zero triggers support the result, while retaining the backstop limited the impact of an unforeseen workload.
  • Evidence snapshot: the latest direct status in this report is timestamped; it should not be mistaken for continuous monitoring after the cutoff.
